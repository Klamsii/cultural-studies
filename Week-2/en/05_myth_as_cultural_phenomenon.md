## 🎯 Essence in 60 Seconds
- **Barthes's Myth Concept (1957):** Myth is a second-order semiotic system built on top of primary language.
- **Denotation vs Connotation:**
  - **Denotation (1st Order):** Direct, literal linguistic meaning of a sign.
  - **Connotation / Myth (2nd Order):** Secondary ideological meaning where the first-order sign becomes a formal signifier for the myth.
- **Central Function of Myth:** **Naturalization of Ideology** — transforming political bourgeois values into "natural laws of common sense."
- **Lotman's Semiosphere & Suleimenov:** Yuri Lotman established culture as a Semiosphere; Olzhas Suleimenov (*AZ i IA*, 1975) uncovered Turkic-Slavic semiotic codes.
- **Tamgaly Petroglyphs:** The Sun-Headed Deity (*Kunhan*) reflects the ancient Eurasian solar sky cult of **Tengri**.

## 📖 Main Content

### 1. Roland Barthes: Myth as a Second-Order Semiotic System (1957)
**Myth appropriates a primary linguistic sign and imposes a secondary ideology.**
Roland Barthes in *Mythologies* (1957) demonstrated that myth operates on two distinct levels:
1. **Denotation (1st Order):** Sign = Signifier (acoustic form) + Signified (literal concept).
2. **Connotation / Myth (2nd Order):** The primary sign becomes a new *Signifier* to express a secondary ideological *Connotation*.
**Example:** A photo of a young soldier saluting a flag:
- *Denotation:* A soldier saluting.
- *Connotation (Myth):* The idea of "great imperial loyalty to the state."

### 2. Main Social Function of Myth: Naturalization of Ideology
**Myth strips away historical political context, presenting norms as "natural common sense."**
The primary function of myth according to Barthes is the **naturalization of ideology** (*naturalization of ideology*). Myth transforms capitalist and class interests into "the natural order of things," stripping viewers of critical awareness.

### 3. Yuri Lotman & Olzhas Suleimenov (AITU 2.3)
**Lotman's Semiosphere and Suleimenov's AZ i IA.**
- **Yuri Lotman:** Formulated the concept of the **Semiosphere** (*Semiosphere*) — the unified space of signs outside of which meanings cannot function.
- **Olzhas Suleimenov (1975):** His masterpiece *AZ i IA* uncovered the bilingual Turkic-Slavic code of "The Song of Igor's Campaign."
