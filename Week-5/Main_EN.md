## 1. Life Cycle Rites: Birth, Maturity and Socialization

### 1.1 Childbirth and Initial Purification (*Shildekhana*, *Besikke salu*)
In Kazakh tradition, childbirth was revered as a sacred gift of Heaven (**Tengri**) under the protection of the fertility goddess **Umay (Umay Ana)**:
- **Shildekhana:** Celebration of childbirth during the first days. Family and community gather to sing songs and protect the newborn from evil spirits.
- **Besikke salu:** Ceremonial placement of the infant into the sacred cradle (*Besik*). An honored grandmother purifies the cradle with adyraspan herb.

### 1.2 Leg-Shackle Cutting Ritual (*Tusau kesu*)
When a toddler takes first steps, the **Tusau kesu** ritual is performed. The child's feet are tied with a black-and-white braided cord (*Ala zhip*), symbolizing the intertwining of good and evil in life. An energetic and respected elder cuts the cord with a knife, wishing a confident walk and bright path in life.

### 1.3 Initiation & Male Socialization (*Sundetke otyrghyzu*, *Batyrs*)
- **Sundetke otyrghyzu:** Circumcision initiation ceremony marking a boy's entry into the male social community.
- **Batyr Estate (*Batyrs*):** Batyrs formed an individual social group with their own ideology, warrior lifestyle, military ethics, and rules of conduct.

## 2. Funeral, Memorial and Wedding Rituals of Nomads

### 2.1 Wedding Cycle: *Uzatu*, *Zhar-zhar*, *Betashar*
- **Uzatu toi:** Bride send-off ceremony from her native parents' home with the farewell song *Koshtasu*.
- **Zhar-zhar:** Choral song in aitys form (antiphonal dialogue between groomsmen and bridesmaids) performed at the wedding celebration.
- **Betashar:** Unveiling of the bride's face at the groom's home. An akyn poet with a dombra unveils her face, introduces her to in-laws, and teaches social etiquette.

### 2.2 Funeral and Memorial Cycle: *Zhoktau*, *As*
- **Zhoktau:** Ritual funeral lament song performed by women of the deceased's family.
- **As:** Commemorative memorial feast held one year after the death of a prominent batyr, bi, or khan, including horse races (*Baige*) and games.

## 3. Music Art, Zhyrau Poetry, Sal-Seri and Traditional Games

### 3.1 Musical Genres & Performance Styles: *Shertpe* vs. *Tokpe*
- **Kyu (Kyui):** Major instrumental musical piece for dombra or kobyz.
- **Shertpe Kyu:** Subtle, finger-plucking performance style. **Tattimbet Kazangapuly (Tattimbet)** is considered the unsurpassed author and performer of shertpe-style kyuis.
- **Tokpe Kyu:** Dynamic, forceful wrist-striking style. **Kurmangazy Sagyrbaev (Kurmangazy)** is the supreme master, author of famous kyuis "Adai" and "Saryarka".

### 3.2 Zhyrau Poetry & The Zar Zaman Movement
- **Zhyrau Poetry:** **Tolgau** is the primary philosophical-didactic poetic genre of the zhyrau poets. **Bukhar zhyrau Kalkamanuly** was the chief adviser and chief poet of Abylai Khan.
- **Zar Zaman Movement ("Era of Lament"):** 19th-century literary movement. **Dulat Babatayuly** (1802–1874) is recognized as the founder/ancestor of the Zar Zaman movement.
- **Jayau Musa Bayzhanuly:** Famous composer and sal-seri poet, author of the protest song **"Ak sisa"**.

### 3.3 Traditional Folk Games & Performance Art (Slides 7–8)
- **Kokpar:** Equestrian sport played over a goat carcass.
- **Asyq atu:** Sheep knuckle-bone game developing precision.
- **Togyzqumalaq:** Strategic board game (steppe chess).
- **Kara Zhorga & Orteke:** Traditional folk dance and puppet musical art.
- Slide 8 draws a comparative parallel to Pieter Bruegel the Elder's *Children's Games* (1560).

## 5. 🔥 Oral Exam Defense: 10 Comprehensive Exam Tickets

#### ❓ Ticket 1: Contrast Shertpe vs. Tokpe kyu styles.
**Answer:** Shertpe is a lyrical finger-plucking style mastered by Tattimbet, while Tokpe is a forceful, rapid wrist-striking style mastered by Kurmangazy.

#### ❓ Ticket 2: Explain the Zhar-zhar wedding song.
**Answer:** Zhar-zhar is a ceremonial choral song performed in an antiphonal aitys format between male and female wedding guests to counsel the bride and groom.
