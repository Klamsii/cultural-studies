## 🎯 Essence in 60 Seconds
- **Dominant Culture:** Cultural patterns supported by the societal majority and dominant state institutions.
- **High vs Popular Culture:** High fine art (opera, classical ballet) vs mass commercial entertainment (pop music, movies, memes).
- **Subculture (Chris Barker, 2012):** A group within mainstream society maintaining distinct jargon, fashion, and values without rejecting dominant society (gamers, K-pop fandoms).
- **Counterculture:** A group actively opposing and seeking to undermine dominant cultural foundations (1960s Hippies, Punks).
- **Evaluative Attitudes:** **Ethnocentrism** (judging other cultures by one's own standards) vs **Cultural Relativism** (evaluating a culture objectively within its own historical context).

## 📖 Main Content

### 1. Dominant Culture, High Culture, and Popular Culture
**Dominant culture shapes the value mainstream of society.**
- **Dominant Culture:** Behavioral patterns, language, and legal codes supported by the majority and state institutions.
- **High Culture:** Elite artistic forms requiring specialized training (symphonic music, opera).
- **Popular Culture:** Mass commercial entertainment (blockbusters, pop music, internet memes).

### 2. Subculture vs Counterculture
**Subcultures coexist with dominant culture, whereas countercultures openly rebel against it.**
- **Subculture (Chris Barker, 2012):** A group maintaining distinct jargon, fashion, and lifestyle (gamers, anime community) without conflicting with state laws.
- **Counterculture:** A group actively rejecting fundamental societal norms and seeking to replace them (1960s anti-war Hippie movement).

### 3. Ethnocentrism vs Cultural Relativism
**Cultural relativism enables studying traditions without bias.**
- **Ethnocentrism:** The tendency to view one's own culture as the benchmark and judge other traditions as "primitive" or "strange."
- **Cultural Relativism:** The principle that cultural practices should be evaluated objectively from within that culture itself.
**Example:** Evaluating the nomadic yurt as a functional eco-dwelling represents cultural relativism.

## ⚖️ Common Misconceptions

| Concept A | Concept B | Key Difference | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Subculture** | **Counterculture** | Coexistence with mainstream vs open rebellion and rejection. | K-pop fans = Subculture; Anarcho-punks = Counterculture. |
| **Ethnocentrism** | **Cultural Relativism** | Judging others by bias vs objective context understanding. | Cultural prejudice = Ethnocentrism; scientific analysis = Relativism. |
