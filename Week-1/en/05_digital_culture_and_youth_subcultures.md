## 🎯 Essence in 60 Seconds
- **Participatory Culture:** Nina Uzelac (2008) characterized the digital environment as a participatory culture, where the boundary between author and passive audience collapses.
- **Prosumers:** Digital network users act simultaneously as producers (*producers*) and consumers (*consumers*) of media content.
- **Digital Subcultures (Chris Barker, 2012):** Barker demonstrated (p. 582) that modern youth subcultures cannot exist purely offline; social networks and digital platforms serve as their core operating infrastructure.

## 📖 Main Content

### 1. Participatory Culture and the Prosumer Figure (Nina Uzelac, 2008)
**The digital network code transformed passive television viewers into active co-creators.**
Nina Uzelac (2008) demonstrated that web technologies created a **Participatory Culture** (*Participatory Culture*). Unlike the screen code (TV), where viewers passively consume broadcast feeds, digital network users act as **prosumers** (*prosumer* = *producer* + *consumer*) — creating blogs, videos, memes, and comments.
**Example:** TikTok or YouTube users do not merely watch videos, but shoot their own trends and write comments.

### 2. Dependency of Youth Subcultures on Digital Networks (Chris Barker, 2012)
**Modern youth subcultures cannot function without digital infrastructure.**
Chris Barker in *Cultural Studies: Theory and Practice* (2012, p. 582) proved that youth subcultures (gamers, K-pop fandoms, esports groups) rely on digital networks as their vital environment for transmitting jargon, fashion, and symbols.
