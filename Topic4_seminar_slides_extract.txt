Madrasah and the Education System in Medieval Central
Asia
The primary schools maktabs grew in number on the steppe and 
became the foundation for students attending higher education in 
madrasas (Frank 2003, 284).  The maktabs usually provide “basic 
literacy through Qur’anic recitation” (Pickett 2020, 110), “Islamic 
catechism, some Arabic prayers, and possibly a degree of literacy 
in Turki” (Frank 2003, 283). Kazakh students received Muslim 
education in Kazakhstani madrasas in Semipalatinsk and 
Petropavlovsk, and many of them attended the madrasas of 
“Troitsk, Astrakhan, and Orenburg’s Seitovskii Posad, as well as in 
smaller communities along the steppe frontier” (Frank 2003, 284). 
studying in Bukhara, “the most prestigious and foremost
destination for study in Central Asia ... attracted students from
throughout the region, not only including Russia, but also India, 
the Qazaq steppe, and Afghanistan” (Frank 2012, 96). 
What influence did these educational institutions have on 
the intellectual and cultural development of the region?
